Accessibility Foundations · Course 2 · Exercise 2

Links, images and color

2 passes · solo or in pairs

The situation

Riverside Parks & Recreation has published its fall registration announcement. It looks finished. It has ten problems, all of them links, images, or color.

  1. Open the page in a new tab. Keep this tab for your answers.
  2. No inspector needed. On the page, choose Show how this page is built. Hover or Tab to any link or image and the bubble shows its text out of context, where it goes, and its alt text.
  3. Pass one, links only. Read every link as though it had been pulled out of its sentence and put in a list, which is what a screen reader user can do.
  4. Pass two, images and color.
  5. Nothing is saved, so copy anything you want to keep before you close the tab. Then open the answer key and compare with the corrected page.

Pass one: links

    “Register (button)”goes to the youth registration form
    “Register (button)”goes to the adult registration form
    “PDF”goes to the scholarship application

Pass two: images and color

  2. The leaf divider has a 45-word description. Should it have any alt text at all? Why?

  4. The infographic is marked decorative. Is that correct? What’s actually needed?

Answer key

Open when you are done

Links

Eight links say nothing on their own. The ninth, the pasted URL, says too much in the wrong way.

ProblemWhy it breaksFix
Three “Click here” links, three destinationsThe links list reads “click here, click here, click here”“Register for a fall program”, “Read the fee and refund policy”, “Find out whether you qualify for a scholarship”
Two “Read more” linksSame problem, and the two are indistinguishable from each other“Volunteer as a youth soccer coach”, “Read the Aquatics Center rules”
Two buttons both reading “Register”Different forms, identical text“Register for a youth program”, “Register for an adult program”
A link whose text is “PDF”Names the format and nothing else“Download the scholarship application (PDF, 240 KB)”
A pasted URL, 78 charactersRead character by character by some screen readers, and it forces horizontal scrolling on a 320px screen because a URL has no break points“Read Indiana’s youth program licensing standards”

On question 3: the raw URL is why the announcement overflows at 320 pixels and the corrected page doesn’t. A long unbroken string can’t wrap. That’s a content decision producing a layout failure, which is a useful thing to be able to say to a developer who assumes overflow is always their problem.

WCAG 2.4.4 Link Purpose in Context (A) · 1.4.10 Reflow (AA)

Images

ImageCategoryAnswer
Hero photo, alt="IMG_4471.jpg"Informative“Swimmers in the outdoor lap pool at the Riverside Aquatics Center.” To be sure yours is right you need to know why the photo is on the page. Here it’s setting the scene for a registration announcement, so identifying the facility is the job. On a page about pool renovations you’d describe the pool itself.
Leaf divider, 45 words of altDecorativeEmpty alt, or the CMS decorative checkbox. Nothing in the page refers to it. The long description is worse than useless: it makes a listener sit through 45 words of nothing.
Program flyer imageText in an imageThe most serious item on the page. Every date, age range, and fee exists only inside the picture. Not searchable, not translatable, not resizable, not readable. The fix is not better alt text. Publish the schedule as a real table, then keep the flyer as a supporting image with brief alt that says the table above has the same information.
Infographic, marked decorativeComplex, and unnecessaryMarking it decorative hides three statistics that appear nowhere else. Three numbers is a sentence: “In 2025 we ran 48 programs, served 2,140 residents, and 31 percent of participants enrolled on a scholarship.” Publish that, then the graphic becomes a visual summary with brief alt.
WCAG 1.1.1 Non-text Content (A) · 1.4.5 Images of Text (AA)

Color

Availability is shown by a green or red dot with a key. That fails WCAG 1.4.1 Use of Color, Level A. Minimum fix: put the word in the text. The corrected page reads “Spots open: Little Sprouts Play Group” and keeps the dot as a redundant visual cue with aria-hidden.

WCAG 1.4.1 Use of Color (A)
